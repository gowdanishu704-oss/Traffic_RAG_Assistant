# Traffic Offences and Fines under the Motor Vehicles Act
Source: Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 as amended by the Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act, 2019 (in force from 1 September 2019), Chapter XIII (Offences, Penalties and Procedure). The amounts below are the central (base) amounts. States can notify different amounts (see "State variations").

## Helmet rule for two-wheelers (Section 194D)
Riding a motorcycle or scooter without a protective helmet, or carrying a pillion rider without one, is punishable under Section 194D read with Section 129. The penalty is a fine of Rs 1,000 and disqualification from holding a driving licence for three months. Section 129 requires every person aged above four years riding a two-wheeler to wear a helmet that conforms to BIS standards. A Sikh wearing a turban is exempt from the helmet requirement.

## Seat belt and child safety (Section 194B)
Driving a motor vehicle without wearing a seat belt, or carrying passengers who are not wearing seat belts, is punishable under Section 194B(1) with a fine of Rs 1,000. Under Section 194B(2), driving with a child below 14 years of age who is not secured by a safety belt or child restraint system is also punishable with a fine of Rs 1,000.

## Overspeeding (Section 183)
Driving faster than the notified speed limit is punishable under Section 183. For a light motor vehicle (car, jeep, taxi) the fine is Rs 1,000 to Rs 2,000. For a medium or heavy passenger or goods vehicle the fine is Rs 2,000 to Rs 4,000. For a second or subsequent offence the driving licence can be impounded.

## Drunk driving (Section 185)
Driving or attempting to drive with more than 30 mg of alcohol per 100 ml of blood (detected by breath analyser or blood test), or under the influence of drugs, is an offence under Section 185. First offence: imprisonment up to six months, or a fine of Rs 10,000, or both. Second or subsequent offence: imprisonment up to two years, or a fine of Rs 15,000, or both. (The 2019 amendment removed the earlier condition that the repeat offence must fall within three years.) The alcohol can be detected by a breath analyser or by any other test, including a laboratory test.

## Dangerous driving, red-light jumping and mobile phone use (Section 184)
Section 184 covers driving dangerously to the public. The 2019 amendment's explanation includes jumping a red light, violating a stop sign, using a handheld communication device (mobile phone) while driving, passing or overtaking unlawfully, and driving against the authorised flow of traffic (wrong-side driving). First offence: imprisonment of six months to one year, or a fine of Rs 1,000 to Rs 5,000, or both. Second or subsequent offence within three years: imprisonment up to two years, or a fine of Rs 10,000, or both.

## Driving without a licence (Sections 3, 180, 181, 182)
Driving without a valid driving licence (Section 3) is punishable under Section 181 with imprisonment up to three months, or a fine of Rs 5,000, or both. An owner who allows an unlicensed person to drive is punishable under Section 180 with imprisonment up to three months, or a fine of Rs 5,000, or both. Driving despite being disqualified from holding a licence is punishable under Section 182 with imprisonment up to three months, or a fine of Rs 10,000, or both.

## Driving without insurance (Section 196)
Driving a vehicle without valid third-party insurance (required by Section 146) is punishable under Section 196. First offence: imprisonment up to three months, or a fine of Rs 2,000, or both. Subsequent offence: imprisonment up to three months, or a fine of Rs 4,000, or both.

## Driving an unregistered vehicle (Section 192)
Driving a vehicle that is not registered (Section 39) is punishable under Section 192. First offence: a fine of Rs 2,000 up to Rs 5,000. Second or subsequent offence: imprisonment up to one year, or a fine of Rs 5,000 up to Rs 10,000, or both.

## Pollution and noise standards / no valid PUC (Section 190(2))
Driving a vehicle that violates air pollution or noise standards, including driving without a valid Pollution Under Control (PUC) certificate, is punishable under Section 190(2) with imprisonment up to three months, or a fine of Rs 10,000, or both, and disqualification from holding a licence for three months.

## Triple riding on a two-wheeler (Section 194C)
Carrying more than one pillion rider on a two-wheeler is punishable under Section 194C with a fine of Rs 1,000 and disqualification from holding a driving licence for three months.

## Not giving way to emergency vehicles (Section 194E)
Failing to give way to an ambulance, fire engine or other notified emergency vehicle is punishable under Section 194E with imprisonment up to six months, or a fine of Rs 10,000, or both.

## Horn and silence zones (Section 194F)
Sounding the horn needlessly, continuously or more than necessary, or in a notified silence zone, is punishable under Section 194F with a fine of Rs 1,000 for the first offence and Rs 2,000 for a subsequent offence.

## Racing and speed trials (Section 189)
Taking part in an unauthorised race or speed trial on a public place is punishable under Section 189. First offence: imprisonment up to three months, or a fine of Rs 5,000, or both. Subsequent offence: imprisonment up to one year, or a fine of Rs 10,000, or both.

## Overloading (Sections 194 and 194A)
Driving a goods vehicle beyond the permissible weight is punishable under Section 194(1) with a fine of Rs 20,000 plus Rs 2,000 for every extra tonne, along with liability to pay charges for offloading the excess load. Carrying passengers beyond the authorised capacity is punishable under Section 194A with a fine of Rs 200 for each excess passenger.

## Offences by juveniles (Section 199A)
If a juvenile (a person below 18 years) commits an offence under the Act, the guardian or owner of the vehicle is deemed guilty. The penalty is imprisonment up to three years and a fine of Rs 25,000. The vehicle's registration is cancelled for 12 months, and the juvenile cannot get a learner's licence or driving licence until the age of 25. The guardian or owner is not liable if they prove that the offence was committed without their knowledge or that they took all due care to prevent it.

## General offences and disobeying orders (Sections 177, 177A, 179)
Any offence under the Act for which no specific penalty is provided is punishable under Section 177 with a fine up to Rs 500 for the first offence and up to Rs 1,500 for a subsequent offence. Violating road regulations made under Section 118 (for example, lane discipline and parking rules) is punishable under Section 177A with a fine of Rs 500 to Rs 1,000. Wilfully disobeying a lawful direction of an authorised person, or obstructing them, is punishable under Section 179(1) with a fine up to Rs 2,000. Wilfully withholding information or giving false information is punishable under Section 179(2) with imprisonment up to one month, or a fine up to Rs 2,000, or both.

## Offences by officials (Section 210B)
If an authority empowered to enforce the Act (for example, a police officer or transport official) commits an offence under the Act, they are liable to twice the penalty for that offence, under Section 210B.

## State variations (Section 210A)
Under Section 210A, a State Government can increase the fines in its State by a multiplier between one and ten. Several States have also notified compounding amounts (on-the-spot fines) under Section 200 that differ from the central amounts. Always check the current notified amount with the State traffic police or the e-challan portal.
