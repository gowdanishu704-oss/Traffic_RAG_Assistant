# What to Do After a Road Accident in India
Source: Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 (as amended in 2019): Sections 134, 134A, 146, 159, 161, 162, 164, 166; Central Motor Vehicles (Fifth Amendment) Rules, 2022 (accident claim procedure); Compensation to Victims of Hit and Run Motor Accidents Scheme, 2022; Cashless Treatment of Road Accident Victims Scheme, 2025; Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023.

## Immediate steps at the accident spot
1. Stop the vehicle and switch on the hazard lights. Do not leave the scene.
2. Check for injuries. Call 112 (national emergency number) or 108 (ambulance) right away.
3. Do not move a seriously injured person unless they are in immediate danger, such as fire or oncoming traffic.
4. Photograph the vehicles, number plates, road conditions and any injuries. Note the names and phone numbers of witnesses.
5. Exchange details with the other party: name, phone number, vehicle number, driving licence and insurance policy.
6. Report the accident to the nearest police station (see the duties under Section 134) and inform your insurance company.

## Legal duty of the driver after an accident (Section 134)
When a person is injured or property is damaged in an accident, the driver or person in charge of the vehicle must:
(a) take all reasonable steps to get medical attention for the injured person, including taking them to the nearest doctor or hospital, unless this is not practicable because of mob fury or other reasons beyond their control;
(b) give any information a police officer asks for and, if no police officer is present, report the accident to the nearest police station as soon as possible, and in any case within 24 hours;
(c) give written information about the accident to the insurer that issued the policy, including the policy number, the date, time and place of the accident, details of the injured or dead, and the name of the driver and their licence details.
Failing to comply is punishable under Section 187 with imprisonment up to six months, or a fine of Rs 5,000, or both. A repeat offence is punishable with imprisonment up to one year, or a fine of Rs 10,000, or both. Section 134 also requires every registered doctor, or the doctor on duty at a hospital, to treat the injured person immediately, without waiting for procedural formalities.

## Good Samaritan protection (Section 134A)
A Good Samaritan is a person who, in good faith and voluntarily, gives emergency medical or non-medical help to a road accident victim at the scene or takes them to hospital. Under Section 134A, a Good Samaritan is not liable for any civil or criminal action if the victim is injured or dies because of negligence in giving that help. A Good Samaritan cannot be forced to reveal their identity or to act as a witness, and hospitals cannot demand payment or a deposit from them. These protections are based on the Supreme Court's 2016 Good Samaritan guidelines.

## Cashless treatment and the golden hour (Section 162)
Section 162 provides for cashless treatment of road accident victims, including during the "golden hour", the first hour after a traumatic injury. Under the Cashless Treatment of Road Accident Victims Scheme, 2025, every victim of a road accident caused by a motor vehicle on any road in India is entitled to cashless treatment of up to Rs 1.5 lakh per victim at designated hospitals, for up to seven days from the date of the accident. Hospitals must stabilise the victim; the payment comes from the Motor Vehicle Accident Fund.

## Hit-and-run compensation (Section 161)
If the vehicle responsible for an accident cannot be identified (hit-and-run), the victim or the family can claim compensation from the Motor Vehicle Accident Fund under Section 161. The compensation is Rs 2,00,000 for death and Rs 50,000 for grievous hurt. Under the Compensation to Victims of Hit and Run Motor Accidents Scheme, 2022, the claim is filed with the Claims Enquiry Officer (usually the Sub-Divisional Magistrate or Tehsildar) of the area where the accident happened, along with a copy of the FIR or accident report.

## Compensation without proving fault (Section 164)
Under Section 164, the owner of the vehicle or the authorised insurer must pay compensation of Rs 5,00,000 for death and Rs 2,50,000 for grievous hurt to the legal heirs or the victim. The claimant does not need to plead or prove that the accident was caused by the wrongful act or negligence of the owner or driver. Any amount received this way is adjusted against compensation awarded later under other provisions.

## Filing a claim with the Motor Accident Claims Tribunal (MACT)
Claims for compensation for death, injury or third-party property damage are decided by the Motor Accident Claims Tribunal (MACT) under Sections 165 and 166. The claim can be filed by the injured person, the property owner, the legal representatives of a deceased victim, or an authorised agent. It can be filed at the Tribunal where the accident happened, where the claimant lives or does business, or where the defendant lives. Under Section 166(3), as amended in 2019, a claim must be filed within six months of the accident.
Under the 2022 claim procedure rules, the police must file a First Accident Report (FAR) with the Claims Tribunal within 48 hours of the accident and a Detailed Accident Report (DAR) within 90 days. The Tribunal can treat the DAR as a claim application.

## Insurance after an accident
Third-party insurance is compulsory for every motor vehicle used in a public place (Section 146). It covers the owner's legal liability for death, injury and property damage caused to others. Damage to your own vehicle is covered only if you have a comprehensive or own-damage policy. After an accident, inform your insurer as soon as possible, because most policies have strict intimation deadlines. Keep a copy of the FIR, the driving licence, the RC, the policy and the repair estimate. Do not move a damaged vehicle for repairs before the insurer's surveyor inspects it, unless the insurer allows it.

## Criminal liability for fatal or rash driving (Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023)
The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023 replaced the Indian Penal Code from 1 July 2024. Section 281 BNS (previously IPC 279) punishes rash or negligent driving on a public way with imprisonment up to six months, or a fine up to Rs 1,000, or both. Section 106(1) BNS (previously IPC 304A) punishes causing death by a rash or negligent act with imprisonment up to five years and a fine. Section 106(2) BNS provides up to ten years' imprisonment and a fine for a driver who causes death by rash and negligent driving and escapes without reporting it to the police or a magistrate. The government put the implementation of Section 106(2) on hold after transporter protests in January 2024.
