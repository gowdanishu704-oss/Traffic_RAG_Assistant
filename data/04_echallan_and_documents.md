# e-Challan, Paying or Contesting Fines, and Vehicle Documents
Source: e-Challan system of the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (echallan.parivahan.gov.in); Virtual Courts (vcourts.gov.in); Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 (Sections 39, 41, 49, 50, 130, 146, 200, 206, 207); Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989 (Rules 115 and 139); MoRTH advisory on digital documents (2018).

## What is an e-challan
An e-challan is an electronic traffic fine issued by traffic police with a handheld device or generated automatically from traffic cameras (red-light, speed and ANPR cameras). It is linked to the vehicle registration number and/or the driving licence and recorded in the national e-Challan system run by MoRTH and NIC. An SMS with the challan number is sent to the mobile number registered with the vehicle.

## How to check pending challans
1. Visit echallan.parivahan.gov.in and choose "Check Online Services" > "Get Challan Details".
2. Enter the challan number, the vehicle registration number, or the driving licence number, and fill in the captcha.
3. The portal lists pending and paid challans, with the offence, location, date and amount. Camera challans usually include a photo as evidence.
Challans can also be checked on the mParivahan app and on many State traffic police websites.

## How to pay a challan
Pay online on echallan.parivahan.gov.in ("Pay Now") using UPI, a debit or credit card, or net banking, and save the receipt. You can also pay in person at a traffic police station or an authorised counter, or pay the officer on the spot through a POS machine (always ask for the receipt). Under Section 200, States notify which offences can be compounded, meaning settled by paying the fine without going to court. If a challan is not paid within the State's deadline, it is sent to a virtual or regular court for judicial disposal.

## How to contest a wrong challan
If you think a challan is wrong (for example, a wrong vehicle number, or the vehicle had been sold or stolen), you can:
1. Raise a grievance on the e-challan portal or the State traffic police grievance portal, attaching evidence.
2. When the challan reaches the Virtual Court (vcourts.gov.in), choose to contest it instead of paying. The case is then sent to a regular court, where you can appear and present your side.
Do not ignore a challan. Unpaid challans can block services such as transfer of ownership, renewal of the RC or licence, and issue of a fitness certificate.

## Lok Adalat for pending challans
National Lok Adalats, organised periodically by the National and State Legal Services Authorities, settle pending traffic challans that have already been sent to court. Fines are often settled at a reduced amount. Lok Adalat dates and token registration are announced by the State Legal Services Authority and traffic police. Offences such as drunk driving, hit-and-run and accidents causing death are generally not settled at Lok Adalats.

## Documents to carry while driving (Section 130)
A driver must produce these documents when a uniformed police officer or authorised officer asks for them:
- Driving licence
- Registration certificate (RC)
- Insurance certificate (third-party insurance at least)
- Pollution Under Control (PUC) certificate
- Fitness certificate and permit (for transport vehicles)
Not producing documents is usually charged as a general offence under Section 177 (fine up to Rs 500 for the first offence). Driving without the document itself, such as without a licence or insurance, attracts the specific penalties under Sections 181 and 196.

## Digital documents through DigiLocker and mParivahan (Rule 139)
Under Rule 139 of the Central Motor Vehicles Rules (amended in 2018) and the MoRTH advisory of 2018, a driving licence, RC, insurance certificate and PUC certificate shown in electronic form through DigiLocker or mParivahan are legally valid. They are equivalent to the original physical documents. Police cannot insist on physical copies when valid digital ones are shown. A scanned photo or a PDF stored in your phone gallery is not a valid replacement; the documents must be fetched through DigiLocker or mParivahan.

## Police powers to seize documents and vehicles (Sections 206 and 207)
Under Section 206, a police officer can impound a document such as a driving licence if they have reason to believe it is fake or that the driver has committed certain offences (for example, overspeeding, drunk driving, or not wearing a helmet), and must give a receipt for it. Under Section 207, a vehicle being driven without registration, a permit, or a valid licence can be detained, and a receipt must be given. Ask for the officer's name and badge number and the receipt for anything seized.

## Pollution Under Control (PUC) certificate
Every motor vehicle must carry a valid PUC certificate (Rule 115 of the Central Motor Vehicles Rules). For a new vehicle, the first PUC certificate is valid for one year from registration. After that, a PUC test is required every six months (validity can differ by vehicle and fuel type and is printed on the certificate). Driving without a valid PUC is punishable under Section 190(2) with a fine up to Rs 10,000.

## Registration certificate (RC) validity and transfer
Under Section 39, a vehicle must be registered before it is driven in a public place. A private (non-transport) vehicle's registration is valid for 15 years from the date of registration and can then be renewed for five years at a time after a fitness check (Section 41). When the address changes, the owner must inform the registering authority within 30 days (Section 49). When a vehicle is sold, the seller must report the transfer within 14 days (or 45 days if the vehicle is transferred to another State), and the buyer must apply for transfer of ownership within 30 days (Section 50). These services are available on the Vahan portal (vahan.parivahan.gov.in).
